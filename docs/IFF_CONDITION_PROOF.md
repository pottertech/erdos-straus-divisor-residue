## 1. The Theorem

**For A an odd positive integer with 4 | (n+A), the z=my parametrization yields a solution to 4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z (with x = (n+A)/4) if and only if:**

**D = n(n+A) has a divisor m satisfying both:**
- **(C1)** `4A | (D/m)(m+1)` — the full divisibility condition
- **(C2)** `m | D` — m is a divisor of D

This decomposes as follows:

### Case 1: A | D

When A divides D, the mod A condition `A | (D/m)(m+1)` can be satisfied via `A | (D/m)` for appropriate m. The condition reduces to finding m | D with `4 | (D/m)(m+1)`.

This is **always satisfiable** when A | D (verified: 0 failures in all cases where A | D).

### Case 2: A ∤ D

When A does not divide D, the condition `A | (D/m)(m+1)` requires `m ≡ -1 (mod A)`.

The mod 4 condition `4 | (D/m)(m+1)` then depends on the parity of m:

- **If m is odd:** m+1 is even, so v₂((D/m)(m+1)) = v₂(D) + v₂(m+1) ≥ 1 + 1 = 2. ✅ **Always satisfied** (since v₂(D) ≥ 1 because n+A is even when n is odd and A is odd).

- **If m is even** with v₂(m) = t: m+1 is odd, so v₂((D/m)(m+1)) = v₂(D) - t. Need v₂(D) - t ≥ 2, i.e., **v₂(m) ≤ v₂(D) - 2**.

### Combined IFF Condition (A ∤ D)

**A works iff D = n(n+A) has a divisor m ≡ -1 (mod A) such that:**
- **m is odd, OR**
- **v₂(m) ≤ v₂(D) - 2**

Equivalently, the only divisors that FAIL are those where:
- m ≡ -1 (mod A), AND
- m is even, AND
- v₂(m) ≥ v₂(D) - 1 (i.e., m consumes almost all the 2-power of D)

## 3. Structural Consequences

### Why odd m always works for mod 4

When m is odd:
- m+1 is even, so v₂(m+1) ≥ 1
- D = n(n+A) where n is odd and n+A is even, so v₂(D) ≥ 1
- v₂((D/m)(m+1)) = v₂(D/m) + v₂(m+1) = v₂(D) + v₂(m+1) ≥ 1 + 1 = 2

So the mod 4 condition is **automatically satisfied** for any odd divisor m ≡ -1 mod A.

### The Type 2 obstruction explained

When all divisors m ≡ -1 (mod A) are even AND have v₂(m) ≥ v₂(D) - 1, the mod 4 condition fails. This happens when:

1. D has QNR mod A factors (so divisors ≡ -1 mod A exist)
2. But all such divisors are even (no odd divisor ≡ -1 mod A exists)
3. AND the even divisors consume too much 2-adic valuation

This is exactly the Type 2 obstruction we identified earlier.

### When does an odd divisor m ≡ -1 mod A exist?

D = n × (n+A) where n is odd and n+A is even.

Odd divisors of D = d₁ × d₂ where d₁ | n (odd) and d₂ | odd_part(n+A) (odd).

The set of achievable residues mod A is:
{d₁ × d₂ mod A : d₁ | n, d₂ | odd_part(n+A)}

This set contains -1 mod A iff -1 is in the subgroup product G₁ × G₂, where:
- G₁ = subgroup of (Z/AZ)* generated by prime factors of n mod A
- G₂ = subgroup of (Z/AZ)* generated by odd prime factors of (n+A) mod A

Since -1 is QNR mod A (when A ≡ 3 mod 4), -1 ∈ G₁ × G₂ iff G₁ × G₂ is not contained in the QR subgroup.

This fails (Type 2 obstruction) when:
- G₁ ⊆ QR (all prime factors of n are QR mod A)
- G₂ ⊆ QR (all odd prime factors of n+A are QR mod A)
- But D has QNR factors — these must be the factor 2 from n+A, or QNR factors only appearing in the full (even) divisors of n+A

Wait — 2 is a prime factor of n+A. Is 2 QR mod A?

- 2 is QR mod p iff p ≡ ±1 mod 8
- 2 is QNR mod p iff p ≡ ±3 mod 8

For A=7: 7 ≡ 7 mod 8, 2 is QR mod 7. So 2 ∈ QR, doesn't help.
For A=11: 11 ≡ 3 mod 8, 2 is QNR mod 11. So 2 IS a QNR factor, but it's even — it only appears in even divisors.
For A=19: 19 ≡ 3 mod 8, 2 is QNR mod 19. Same.
For A=23: 23 ≡ 7 mod 8, 2 is QR mod 23.

So for A=11 and A=19, the factor 2 itself is a QNR, but it only appears in even divisors, creating the Type 2 obstruction!

## 4. The Two Obstructions Summarized

For A ∤ D, A ≡ 3 mod 4 prime:

### Type 1: No QNR factor in odd part of D
All odd prime factors of D = n(n+A) are QR mod A. The factor 2 (from n+A) might be QNR mod A (when A ≡ 3 mod 8), but 2 only appears in even divisors. No odd divisor ≡ -1 mod A exists.

### Type 2: QNR factors exist but all valid divisors are even with excessive 2-adic valuation
D has QNR mod A factors (possibly including 2 itself), so divisors ≡ -1 mod A exist, but all such divisors are even with v₂(m) ≥ v₂(D) - 1, consuming too much 2-power for mod 4 to work.

### No other obstructions exist (verified computationally)

## 5. Implications for A-Boundedness

The iff condition shows that A=p fails iff:

**Every divisor m ≡ -1 (mod p) of D = n(n+p) is either:**
- **Even with v₂(m) ≥ v₂(D) - 1**

This is a condition on the interaction between:
1. The quadratic residue structure of D mod p
2. The 2-adic valuation structure of D

For the Chinese Remainder sieve approach:
- Different primes p probe different QR structures
- The 2-adic constraint is shared across all p (it depends on v₂(n+p))
- As p varies, v₂(n+p) changes, so the 2-adic constraint shifts

This suggests the sieve approach could work: for different A values, the 2-adic structure changes (since n+A has different v₂ for different A), potentially escaping the obstruction.

## 6. Next Steps

1. **Prove the iff condition rigorously** — the computational verification is strong evidence, but a formal proof would show:
   - (⇒) If A works, the divisor m satisfies the condition (trivial from the algebra)
   - (⇐) If the condition holds, m yields a valid solution (direct construction)

2. **Characterize when an odd divisor ≡ -1 mod A exists** — This is a problem in the multiplicative structure of (Z/AZ)* restricted to odd divisors of D.

3. **Show the sieve terminates** — For any n, show there exists bounded A such that the iff condition is satisfied, using the fact that different A values probe different QR and 2-adic structures.

4. **Handle the A | D case formally** — Show A | D always gives a working solution (mod 4 is always satisfiable).
